# Politangle School — Question inventory (English)

Source: `main`, 11 November 2026. Candidate wording; not validated for real-school deployment. For BELIEVE, two opposing wordings are alternate forms of the **same** question, not simultaneous selectable options. A pupil sees one wording and chooses a response on the agreement scale.

## Junior BELIEVE (ages 10–13) (16)

### J01

- Statement A: Government should make sure every family can use schools and doctors.
- Statement B: Families should make more of their own choices, with less government help.

### J02

- Statement A: Taxes should help make the gap between rich and poor smaller.
- Statement B: People should keep more of what they earn, even if the gap stays large.

### J03

- Statement A: Workers or the public should own more of the biggest businesses.
- Statement B: The biggest businesses should mostly belong to private owners.

### J04

- Statement A: Public rules should change when most people’s views change.
- Statement B: Old rules should change slowly, after people have agreed for a long time.

### J05

- Statement A: Adults should be free to make private choices if nobody is harmed.
- Statement B: Some private choices may be limited to protect values shared by many people.

### J06

- Statement A: Leaders should limit freedom only when there is a clear, serious danger.
- Statement B: Leaders may limit some freedom early to stop serious trouble.

### J07

- Statement A: Courts, laws, other parties and news organisations should check elected leaders.
- Statement B: Election winners should have wide freedom to do what voters chose.

### J08

- Statement A: Countries should follow shared rules for problems that cross borders.
- Statement B: Each country should keep the final say, even if shared problems are harder to solve.

### J09

- Statement A: Someone who becomes a citizen belongs just as fully as someone who was a citizen from birth.
- Statement B: Being a citizen from birth should count more when deciding who fully belongs.

### J10

- Statement A: Politics usually has many groups with different needs and ideas.
- Statement B: Political decisions can give too much influence to well-connected groups and too little to ordinary voters.

### J11

- Statement A: Protecting nature should sometimes come before growing the economy.
- Statement B: Growing the economy should usually come first while we also protect nature.

### J12

- Statement A: Religion should not be used to make laws for people who do not share it.
- Statement B: Religion can be a fair reason for laws, even when not everyone shares it.

### J13

- Statement A: National or regional government should run key services when that is needed to guarantee the same minimum standard.
- Statement B: Local communities should run key services whenever they can provide them well.

### J14

- Statement A: If violence broke out, I would keep strict limits on police powers.
- Statement B: If violence broke out, I would give police more power for a short time.

### J15

- Statement A: If a court stopped a popular policy, I would still protect the court’s independence.
- Statement B: If courts often stopped legal policies voters chose, I would give elected leaders more freedom.

### J16

- Statement A: I would listen to a candidate who explains different groups’ needs, even when the answer is complicated.
- Statement B: I prefer a candidate who says well-connected groups have too much influence and ordinary voters need more say.

## Youth BELIEVE (ages 14–18; Quick 26 / Full 42) (42)

### T01 — Quick

- Statement A: The government should make sure everyone can use things like schools and doctors.
- Statement B: The government should do less when it comes to things like schools and doctors. More of these should be run by private companies, and people should choose for themselves.

### T02 — Quick

- Statement A: The government should use taxes and financial help to shrink very big gaps between rich and poor people.
- Statement B: The government should step in less on differences in how much money people have, even when the gaps get very big.

### T03 — Quick

- Statement A: Workers or the public should own a bigger share of large companies.
- Statement B: Big companies should mostly be owned by private owners, not the government. The law should still protect workers.

### T04 — Quick

- Statement A: When most people start to accept a new way of living, the laws should change quite quickly to match.
- Statement B: Old rules should change only after most people have accepted a new way of living for a long time.

### T05 — Quick

- Statement A: The law should leave adults alone in their private choices when nobody else is hurt.
- Statement B: The law can ban some private things that adults choose to do, if most people think they are morally wrong, even when nobody gets hurt.

### T06 — Quick

- Statement A: Abortion (ending a pregnancy) should mostly be legal, because the pregnant person should make the main decision.
- Statement B: Abortion should have stronger legal limits, because unborn life should get more protection.

### T07 — Quick

- Statement A: The government should limit freedom only when it can show a clear and serious risk of harm.
- Statement B: The government can limit some freedoms early, before anything bad happens, if there is a real risk of serious violence or chaos.

### T08 — Quick

- Statement A: A government that wins an election should still be kept in check by courts, the opposition and the country’s basic rules (the constitution).
- Statement B: An elected government should be free to carry out its legal plans, even when courts and other independent groups strongly oppose them.

### T09 — Quick

- Statement A: Countries should follow shared international rules when a problem is too big for one country to solve alone.
- Statement B: Each country should have the final say on its own decisions, even if that makes it harder to solve shared problems together.

### T10 — Quick

- Statement A: A person who becomes a citizen later in life belongs to the country just as much as a person who was born a citizen.
- Statement B: Being a citizen from birth is an important part of belonging to a country, more than legal citizenship alone.

### T11 — Quick

- Statement A: Political disagreements are usually real clashes between different interests and values, not one side betraying ordinary voters.
- Statement B: Political decisions often give too much influence to well-connected groups (groups that know the right people) and too little to ordinary voters.

### T12 — Quick

- Statement A: When protecting nature and growing the economy clearly clash, nature should sometimes come first, even if the economy grows more slowly.
- Statement B: When protecting nature and growing the economy clash, growing the economy should usually come first, and harm to nature should be reduced in other ways.

### T13 — Quick

- Statement A: Religious beliefs should not be enough, on their own, to justify laws for people who do not share that religion.
- Statement B: Laws can be based on religious values, even if some people in the country do not share that religion.

### T14 — Quick

- Statement A: The national or regional government should run important services, like schools and hospitals, if that is the best way to give everyone the same access and quality.
- Statement B: Important services, like schools and hospitals, should usually stay with local government or community groups when they can run them well.

### F01 — Quick

- Statement A: It bothers me when people cannot get important things like healthcare because it depends too much on what they can pay for privately.
- Statement B: It bothers me when government control of important services leaves people with too little choice.

### F02 — Quick

- Statement A: Very big gaps between rich and poor bother me enough that I think some people should pay higher taxes.
- Statement B: High taxes worry me, even when the aim is to make the gap between rich and poor smaller.

### F03 — Quick

- Statement A: It feels fair when workers or the public own a real share of big companies.
- Statement B: It feels fair when private investors keep most of the ownership of big companies, as long as the law protects workers.

### F04 — Quick

- Statement A: I feel uncomfortable when the rules stay far behind a change that most people support.
- Statement B: I feel uncomfortable when old rules change before most people have accepted a new way of living.

### F05 — Quick

- Statement A: I feel uncomfortable when the government stops adults from making private choices that do not hurt anyone else.
- Statement B: I feel uncomfortable when the law pays no attention to the values most people share, even in private adult life.

### F06 — Quick

- Statement A: Limits on abortion worry me, because they can take an important decision away from the pregnant person.
- Statement B: Wide access to abortion worries me, because I believe unborn life deserves more protection.

### F07 — Quick

- Statement A: In a crisis, it worries me when the government uses more power than it needs.
- Statement B: In a crisis, it worries me when the authorities have too little power to keep people safe.

### F08 — Quick

- Statement A: It worries me when the winners of an election weaken the checks on their own power.
- Statement B: I worry when independent courts often cancel laws that an elected parliament has passed.

### F09 — Quick

- Statement A: I feel uncomfortable when shared problems stay unsolved because countries refuse agreements they would have to follow.
- Statement B: I feel uncomfortable when international promises stop my country from making its own final decision on an important issue.

### F10 — Quick

- Statement A: I feel the same sense of belonging with citizens who joined later and take part in community life as with people who were citizens from birth.
- Statement B: It matters to my feeling of belonging to a country that my family has lived there for a long time.

### F11 — Quick

- Statement A: When politics lets me down, I usually think the main reason is that the choices are hard and different groups want different things.
- Statement B: When politics lets me down, I often suspect that well-connected groups are heard more than ordinary voters.

### F12 — Quick

- Statement A: Serious damage to nature worries me, even when stopping it could slow down the economy.
- Statement B: I worry about lost jobs and lower living standards when environmental rules slow down the economy a lot.

### F13 — Full only

- Statement A: I feel uneasy when religious values are used to justify laws for people who do not share that faith.
- Statement B: I feel uneasy when religious values are brushed aside in public debate just because they are religious.

### F14 — Full only

- Statement A: I feel confident when the national or regional government sets the same minimum standard for important services everywhere.
- Statement B: I feel confident when local authorities, like a town council, keep control instead of handing decisions to the region or the national government.

### A01 — Full only

- Statement A: If a local hospital might close, I would support public money to keep it open for everyone.
- Statement B: If a local hospital would work well either way, I would choose a private company to run it instead of giving it more public money.

### A02 — Full only

- Statement A: Suppose I earn a salary and pay taxes. I would accept paying a bit more tax if it made the gap between rich and poor much smaller.
- Statement B: Suppose I earn a salary and pay taxes. I would choose lower taxes, even if the gap between rich and poor stayed very big.

### A03 — Full only

- Statement A: If a big company gave its workers a real ownership share, even if outside shareholders lost some control, I would support it.
- Statement B: If a company were doing well, I would keep it mainly with private shareholders (people who own parts of it) rather than give workers or the public a big share.

### A04 — Full only

- Statement A: If an old school rule no longer matched most students’ lives, I would change it fairly soon.
- Statement B: I would keep an old school rule until many people had wanted to change it for a long time.

### A05 — Full only

- Statement A: If adults freely chose a relationship or way of life that I disagree with, I would be against a law banning it, unless it directly harmed someone else.
- Statement B: If an adult choice went strongly against values most people share, I could support some legal limits, even if nobody was hurt.

### A06 — Full only

- Statement A: If people voted on abortion laws, I would vote for wider legal access.
- Statement B: If people voted on abortion laws, I would vote for tighter legal limits.

### A07 — Full only

- Statement A: If there were serious violence in the streets, I would keep strong legal protections, even if the response was slower.
- Statement B: If there were serious violence in the streets, I would give the government more power for a short time, even if some legal protections were weakened.

### A08 — Full only

- Statement A: If an independent court cancelled a law I supported, I would still respect its decision.
- Statement B: If independent courts kept cancelling laws passed by parliament, I would consider limiting some of their powers.

### A09 — Full only

- Statement A: I would support an agreement that all countries must follow if it solved a shared problem, even if it limited some of my country’s choices.
- Statement B: I would keep decisions in my own country’s hands, even if that made it harder for countries to act together.

### A10 — Full only

- Statement A: I would support letting people who became citizens later in life hold the same high elected jobs, like leading the country, as people who were born citizens.
- Statement B: I would support keeping some top elected jobs, like leading the country, for people who were citizens from birth.

### A11 — Full only

- Statement A: I prefer candidates who explain openly that political choices are hard, even when their message is less exciting.
- Statement B: I prefer candidates who promise to reduce the power of well-connected groups and give ordinary voters more say.

### A12 — Full only

- Statement A: I would support laws that force companies to protect the environment, even if the economy grew a little more slowly.
- Statement B: I would oppose environmental rules that slow growth on purpose, and prefer ways to protect nature that keep the economy growing.

### A13 — Full only

- Statement A: I would prefer politicians not to rely mainly on religious values when they explain laws that apply to everyone.
- Statement B: I would accept lawmakers giving religious values as a good reason for laws that apply to everyone.

### A14 — Full only

- Statement A: If services like schools were much better in some areas than in others, I would support national or regional standards, even if local areas had less control.
- Statement B: If local services worked well, I would keep decisions local, even if national rules would make services more alike.

### SU1

- A. It is mainly a campaign style, so it has no recurring claim about who should hold political power.

### SU2

- B. It tells you nationhood matters politically, but you still need economic and social views to place the wider ideology.
