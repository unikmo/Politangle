# Academic validation of headline-family loadings

Status: **evidence-informed validation prior; not psychometrically calibrated**.

This document validates the 14 BELIEVE constructs against the five headline Politangle families before respondent calibration. It is intentionally conservative: an academically observed association is **not automatically promoted into a scoring loading**.

## Rule for 0 / 1 / 2

- `2` = defining/core for the broad family and strongly supported by academic/reference literature.
- `1` = relevant/typical but variable across strands, countries or periods.
- `0` = do not use in the headline family score. This does **not** mean the family has no relationship to the issue; it means the relationship is too variable, too narrow, duplicated by a more fundamental construct, or better treated as a cross-cutting tendency/subtype.

Directions refer to the canonical BELIEVE poles:
- `-` = negative/first pole
- `+` = positive/second pole

## Rationale by family

### Liberalism

**Core:** liberty/personal autonomy, civil liberties and liberal-democratic constraints. SEP treats liberty as the unifying liberal value, while the Manifesto family profile emphasizes political/civil liberties, personal choice and privacy.

**Ownership = 1 toward private ownership:** SEP explicitly identifies private property and market order as a crucial liberal fault line. Classical liberalism strongly links liberty and private property, while new/welfare liberalism weakens that connection. Therefore ownership is relevant but not defining for the entire broad Liberal family.

**Abortion remains 0:** liberal parties often support abortion rights and CHES includes abortion within the libertarian/postmaterialist side of GAL-TAN, but Politangle already measures the deeper autonomy construct. Making abortion an additional Liberal family loading would risk double-counting a country-sensitive issue.

**World/sovereignty and religion remain 0:** international cooperation and secularism are common in liberal party profiles, but are not sufficiently universal across the whole liberal tradition to carry the headline-family score.

### Conservatism

**Core:** continuity/tradition. SEP describes living tradition, cautious reform and skepticism toward abstract or rapid change as central. The Manifesto handbook likewise emphasizes status quo, customs and institutions.

**Ownership = 1 toward private ownership:** SEP says the conservative organic social vision places faith in family, private property and religion, while also stressing that Conservatism is not reducible to a single economic doctrine. The Manifesto family profile identifies free markets and private property as typical. Christian-democratic social-market traditions confirm why this should be relevant but not defining.

**Authority/order = 1:** both SEP and the Manifesto family profile support a stronger role for authority/law and order, but this is not sufficient to define Conservatism by itself.

**Pluralism = 0:** competitive/pluralist democracy can coexist with Conservatism, but it is not a defining Conservative criterion. Keeping it out of the headline score also prevents the engine from confusing broad Conservatism with the separate cross-cutting democratic-constraints dimension. Radical/far-right nuance remains responsible for stronger nativist + authoritarian + populist combinations.

**Public provision and redistribution remain 0:** Conservative and Christian-democratic traditions include substantial variation in welfare, regulation and state intervention. Supporting universal healthcare or redistribution should therefore not automatically count against broad Conservatism.

### Social democracy

Routledge defines modern social democracy as a predominantly capitalist economy with regulation, welfare services and redistribution for social justice. The Manifesto handbook identifies welfare-state expansion, mixed economy, redistribution and representative democracy as central programmatic features.

Therefore:
- public provision = `2 −`
- redistribution = `2 −`
- ownership = `1 +` because the economy remains predominantly capitalist/mixed rather than generally socialized
- pluralism = `2 −` because representative democracy is integral to the modern tradition

Culture-war or nationhood questions remain outside the headline score because they vary across countries and are not needed to distinguish the family economically/institutionally.

### Socialism

SEP defines the contrast with capitalism primarily through **social/democratic control of the bulk of the means of production**. That makes ownership the strongest diagnostic construct.

Therefore:
- ownership = `2 −`
- public provision = `1 −`
- redistribution = `1 −`

Redistribution is deliberately weaker than ownership because extensive redistribution can occur within capitalism and therefore should not by itself imply Socialism.

Pluralism remains `0` for the broad Socialist family because socialist traditions contain materially different institutional designs. Democratic-socialist nuance can surface only when socialist economic signals coexist with democratic/pluralist signals.

### Green politics

Cambridge identifies ecological limits/sustainable society as the distinctive Green core, commonly connected with grassroots democracy, decentralisation, social justice and non-violence. The Manifesto ecological-family profile similarly emphasizes environmental protection, welfare expansion, subsidiarity/regionalism, participatory democracy, minority politics and international cooperation.

Therefore:
- ecology/growth = `2 −`
- redistribution/social justice = `1 −`
- authority/order = `1 −`
- pluralism = `1 −`
- world/sovereignty = `1 −`
- subsidiarity = `1 +`

Ownership remains `0` because Green politics contains market-oriented, social-democratic, ecosocialist and other economic strands; ownership is not a universal Green diagnostic.

## Constructs intentionally kept out of all five headline scores

### Nationhood / citizenship-at-birth

Citizenship rules and national belonging vary across countries and ideological hosts. Politangle therefore uses the construct primarily in the Nationalism tendency rather than forcing it into Liberalism or Conservatism.

### Populism

Populism is thin-centered/cross-cutting and can attach to left, right, progressive, conservative or socialist host ideologies. It remains a separate tendency.

### Abortion

There are clear left/right and party-family associations in many countries, but the issue is also religiously and nationally contingent. Politangle measures it in the political shape without making it a headline-family discriminator.

### Religion in public policy

Religion is decisive for Christian-democratic/religious-conservative nuance, but broad Conservatism and the other headline families are too internally diverse for this item to carry their headline score.

## Red Team / limitations

The strongest challenge to this matrix is that **party families are not perfectly coherent ideological objects**. De La Cerda & Gunderson show substantial convergence among some center-right families and internal heterogeneity among left families, while also warning about East/West differences. This argues for *fewer* deterministic loadings, not more.

Accordingly:

- `0` must never be described as “the family has no view”.
- `1` and `2` are content-validation priors, not psychometric discrimination coefficients.
- issue-specific associations should not be added merely because contemporary parties correlate with them.
- respondent calibration must later test sensitivity, item discrimination, factor structure and cross-country invariance.
- a respondent may score strongly on more than one headline family.

**Verdict: TEST WITH CONDITIONS.** The matrix is sufficiently evidence-backed for a pilot candidate, but not for claims of scientific diagnostic accuracy.
