# Politangle engine — Red Team review

Date: 2026-09-07

Decision: **TEST FIRST / CONFIRM WITH CONDITIONS**.

This critique is intentionally based on evidence that challenges overconfident ideological measurement, not only on sources that support the current model.

## 1. Most fragile assumption

The most fragile assumption is that Politangle's four Quick dimensions plus seven Deep belief dimensions will summarize political attitudes in a meaningfully comparable way across countries and populations.

There is good evidence that political ideology is multidimensional, but that does not validate this exact set of dimensions or the current items. Research on left-right placement also shows cross-national variation in what political labels and response scales mean.

Contrary/limiting evidence reviewed:

- Carroll & Kubo, *Measuring and explaining the complexity of left-right perceptions of political parties*, Electoral Studies 71 (2021), DOI 10.1016/j.electstud.2021.102310: the dimensionality underlying left-right party perceptions varies cross-nationally.
- Lin & Lee, *Anchoring Vignettes as a Diagnostic Tool for Cross-National (in)Comparability of Survey Measures*, British Journal of Political Science (2023): left-right self-placement can exhibit differential item functioning across countries.
- *Lingua Franca of Ideology? Common and Country-Specific Measures of Symbolic and Operational Ideology in Asian and Western Countries*, Public Opinion Quarterly (2026): the meaning and relevance of left-right labels varies across national contexts, especially beyond Western Europe.
- Schemer et al., *Measuring Populism across Nations*, International Journal of Public Opinion Research 32(2): cross-national use of populism measures requires explicit invariance, reliability and validity testing rather than assuming portability.

## 2. What evidence would contradict the current design

The model should be revised if pilot data show any of the following:

- Quick items do not recover four reasonably separable dimensions;
- Deep axes show low internal coherence or collapse into other constructs;
- respondents systematically interpret paired alternatives differently from the intended construct;
- high “Not sure / I do not understand” rates cluster on specific items or groups;
- item functioning differs materially by country, language, age or education;
- modeled tradition distinctions do not align with external expert or self-description validation tasks;
- social democracy and democratic socialism cannot be separated reliably through the ownership items;
- nativism, populism and authority do not behave as distinct signals in the target population.

## 3. Reversible test before hard-coding public claims

The lowest-cost reversible test is:

1. cognitive interviews on the paired Quick and Deep belief items;
2. a small pilot with item-level diagnostics;
3. expert review of every literacy answer key and every tradition criterion;
4. only then lock the next questionnaire/scoring version;
5. collect a larger validation sample before claims of psychometric validity or cross-national comparability.

Versioning is already built into the engine, so weak items or criteria can be replaced without pretending historical results were generated by the same model.

## 4. Downside if the model is wrong

The main failure is not a small scoring error. It is a credibility and neutrality failure: users could be told that their answers are compatible with a political tradition for reasons that are culturally narrow, empirically weak or conceptually conflated.

The highest-risk examples are:

- treating nationalism as automatically right-wing or fascist;
- treating welfare-state preferences as socialism or communism;
- treating ordinary conservative/social-traditional views as radical-right evidence;
- treating populism as a complete left/right ideology;
- treating procedural majoritarianism as identical to explicit anti-democracy;
- presenting precise ideology percentages where no empirical calibration exists.

The current model includes explicit safeguards against these shortcuts, but the safeguards themselves need validation.

## 5. Preserve-first alternative

If empirical validation is weaker than expected, preserve:

- the 26-item multidimensional Quick experience;
- explicit, separately reported dimensions;
- the belief-versus-literacy separation;
- evidence-backed explanations;
- versioning and uncertainty/coverage reporting.

Reduce or postpone:

- named-tradition compatibility;
- any extreme/far-right pattern output;
- cross-country comparisons;
- precise similarity scores or percentile claims.

This produces a useful political-literacy tool even if the ideology-compatibility layer proves too unstable.

## 6. Dependence on unavailable data

Politangle currently has no respondent dataset with which to estimate:

- factor structure;
- item discrimination;
- reliability;
- criterion validity;
- measurement invariance;
- empirical tradition weights;
- population norms or percentiles.

Therefore every current score is a transparent rule-based content-validation output rather than an empirically calibrated diagnostic.

## 7. Release condition

Do not change the public claim from “content-validation / exploratory” to “validated political diagnostic” until empirical validation evidence exists and has been independently reviewed.

For schools, any use involving minors or individual political-opinion data also **REQUIRES QUALIFIED LEGAL REVIEW** before production.
