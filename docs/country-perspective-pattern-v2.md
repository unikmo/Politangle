# Politangle country perspective pattern v2

**Status:** required editorial pattern for every country perspective  
**Scope:** all countries and all supported country-page locales

A country perspective must use the same **information architecture** without becoming a generic template. The structure is shared; the political substance must remain country-specific.

## Required pattern

Every canonical country profile must contain:

1. **At a glance** — at least five institutional facts.
2. **Three things to know first** — exactly three substantive explanations of how political power actually works in that country.
3. **Global-label fit** — one of `strong`, `partial`, or `limited`, followed by:
   - a country-specific explanation of how far shared Politangle dimensions travel; and
   - at least four local dimensions that should not be collapsed into left/right.
4. **Local political vocabulary** — exactly two concepts whose meaning is genuinely useful for reading that country's politics.
5. **Turning points** — at least four political or constitutional events that help explain the present institutional order.
6. **Sources** — at least two primary, official, constitutional, parliamentary, electoral or otherwise authoritative institutional sources.
7. **Current snapshot** — optional and shown only where separately verified. Absence must remain explicit rather than being filled from stale or inferred data.

## Global-label fit

The fit label describes the usefulness of shared comparative dimensions; it is **not a rating of the country or its political system**.

- **strong** — familiar economic/social ideological families are a substantial organising language of party and policy competition, although local dimensions still matter.
- **partial** — shared dimensions are useful, but one or more independent constitutional, territorial, identity, historical, religious, security or coalition cleavages are essential to understanding politics.
- **limited** — a conventional competitive left/right party spectrum would materially misdescribe the system or the main logic of political alignment. Shared issue dimensions may still be used issue by issue.

## Country-specificity rule

The following is prohibited:

- copying another country's prose and swapping institution or country names;
- generic statements that could be pasted onto many countries unchanged;
- forcing local political movements into a global ideological label when the fit is weak;
- treating a party name as sufficient evidence of ideological placement;
- turning clan, ethnic, religious, territorial, security, constitutional or patronage cleavages into left/right solely for visual consistency.

Every country must contain at least one nationally distinctive institutional mechanism, historical concept, party-system feature or political term in the explanatory sections.

## Locale rule

The locale versions preserve the same verified facts and the same required pattern, but explanatory prose should read naturally in the target language. Institutional labels and chronology may necessarily be close translations; explanatory paragraphs should not be machine-like sentence-by-sentence mirrors when the target language would normally explain the point differently.

## Regression rule

Tests must fail if:

- the country count and native-locale route count diverge;
- any country misses a required section or falls below the minimum depth;
- global-label fit is absent;
- a country-specific anchor disappears;
- generic scaffold phrases return;
- a localized page silently falls back to English for country-specific explanatory content.
