# Politangle School Pilot — Teacher Guide v1

Status: **pilot resource**. Politangle School is not yet a broad-production product for minors. The 10–13 experience must be cognitively tested with real students and the School product requires qualified legal/privacy review before broad deployment.

## Why this belongs in a student's toolkit

**Political literacy starts long before voting age.** Students already meet political claims in social feeds, videos, group chats, family conversations, advertising, news and AI-generated content. Waiting until a young person can vote is too late to begin teaching how political language and persuasion work.

Politangle School is designed to give students a structured, non-partisan place to practise four skills:

1. **Check before reacting.** Separate a claim from its evidence, the emotion it creates and the attempt to persuade.
2. **Know what the words mean.** Do not treat welfare, socialism, communism, populism, nationalism, democracy and authoritarianism as interchangeable labels.
3. **Understand democratic power.** See elections, rights, rule of law, opposition and independent checks as related but distinct parts of democratic government.
4. **Disagree without caricature.** Be able to state the strongest fair reason for a different view before criticizing it.

The product is not designed to tell students what political position to hold. For BELIEVE questions the student is told plainly: **There is no correct political answer.** For knowledge questions, the product teaches evidence-backed definitions and distinctions.

## Privacy rule

> **The teacher sees the room, never the individual student.**

Students join an active classroom with a temporary room code. The classroom flow does not require a student name, email address, username, avatar, school ID or roster. Teachers receive class-level response totals and distributions, not a mapping from a student to an answer, an individual political profile or an individual political-literacy score.

A teacher pilot request must contain adult/school contact information only. **Do not enter student names or student data in the pilot form.**

## What teachers can see before registering

The public Teacher area intentionally shows the resource catalogue before access is granted so a teacher can decide whether Politangle is relevant to the class. The preview shows lesson title, intended age band, estimated duration and learning-goal scope.

The complete lesson flow, discussion prompts, live classroom controls and class-summary tools remain gated. A teacher first submits the pilot request and receives an approved teacher access code before those operational resources open.

This is a deliberate pilot gate. It lets us control minor-facing rollout while we test comprehension, classroom dynamics, privacy expectations and teacher usability with real schools.

## Ages 10–13 — Junior

Junior is a separate product form, not the older Youth questionnaire with a smaller font.

### Junior BELIEVE

- 16 single-statement political-perspective questions;
- 13 constructs represented;
- short wording with automated reading-load and jargon guardrails;
- full-word choices rather than numeric survey shorthand;
- an explicit **I'm not sure / I don't understand** option;
- no individual political profile sent to the teacher.

### Junior political know-how

Junior has its own eight-question knowledge bank rather than using the older Youth bank. It covers socialism and ownership, the Marxian communist ideal, elections plus rights and checks, public funding versus ownership, why one state-owned company does not make a country communist, why national self-government does not by itself tell you left or right, a basic definition of populism, and why a large welfare state does not automatically make a country socialist.

Prompts and answer choices are intentionally short and avoid specialist terms such as *subsidiarity*, *pluralism*, *nativism*, *majoritarianism* and *means of production*. A teacher can introduce formal vocabulary after the underlying idea is understood.

### Junior guided lessons

**Who is trying to influence me? — 45 min**

Students compare evidence with emotionally persuasive messages, learn to notice blame and oversimplified us-versus-them framing, and practise a pause–check–compare routine before sharing a claim.

**Power, fairness and disagreement — 45 min**

Students explore freedom, safety, fairness, elections and limits on power. They practise giving a fair reason for both sides of a disagreement rather than treating disagreement as a personal conflict.

## Ages 14–18 — Youth

Youth may use Quick 26, Full 42, political-literacy questions, guided lesson packs, teacher-paced or student-paced classroom activities, and selected single questions/custom activities.

The Youth wording is separate from the adult source form while preserving the source scoring coordinates. It remains a versioned candidate form until validation is complete.

## Supported interactive languages

The current interactive School implementation supports **English, German, Spanish and French**.

Student join/privacy copy, Junior response choices, Junior BELIEVE wording, Junior knowledge questions, the public literacy question bank, School landing/private routes, teacher access/pilot flow, teacher dashboard controls and the classroom projector have language-specific content in these four languages.

**Current documentation limitation:** the long-form legacy teacher handbook PDFs and every detailed line of the older Youth lesson-guide prose have not yet been professionally translated into all four languages. Do not describe the documentation library as fully localized until that pass is complete.

## How to run an 11-year-old pilot

A code-level reading test is not the same as comprehension by a real 11-year-old. Use the pilot to find wording that adults think is simple but children interpret differently.

For each selected Junior item:

1. Ask the student to read the item without coaching.
2. Ask: **“What do you think this question is asking?”** Do not repeat the intended meaning first.
3. Ask the student to explain the answer choices in their own words.
4. Note any word that needs adult explanation.
5. Ask whether two answer choices seem to mean the same thing.
6. Record whether the student uses **“I'm not sure / I don't understand”** because of uncertainty about the politics or because the wording is unclear.
7. Ask whether the question felt as though there was a “good” or “bad” answer.
8. After submission, ask the student to explain the privacy promise: **what can the teacher see and what can the teacher not see?**
9. Record completion time and where attention drops.
10. Invite the student to rewrite one confusing sentence in their own words.

### Revision triggers

A Junior item should be revised before broader use when several students paraphrase it as a different question, a key word repeatedly needs adult explanation, students cannot distinguish the response choices, wording makes one political position sound morally approved by the product, the item requires knowledge the question is supposed to measure as a personal perspective, the student misunderstands what the teacher can see, or a question creates avoidable distress without educational necessity.

Do not lower the bar because a child eventually understands after an adult explains the intended answer. The first interpretation is part of the test.

## Teacher observation checklist

During a pilot lesson, record aggregate observations rather than political notes about named students: join success, vocabulary questions, “I don't understand” use, understanding of BELIEVE versus knowledge questions, whether live distributions improve discussion or create peer pressure, whether manual reveal reduces conformity pressure, and actual lesson time.

## Release gates before broad 10–13 rollout

Broad Junior deployment should remain gated until all of the following are complete:

1. cognitive interviews with intended 10–13-year-old users in the supported deployment context;
2. teacher-led classroom pilots across more than one class/school context;
3. accessibility and mobile-usability testing;
4. review of translations with fluent educators, not only literal-language checks;
5. qualified legal/privacy review for use with minors and schools;
6. documented revision of any item that fails the comprehension or neutrality criteria above.

Automated tests can protect length, vocabulary, routing, privacy boundaries and scoring behavior. They cannot establish that an eleven-year-old interprets a political concept exactly as intended.

## Pilot registration and approval flow

1. Teacher opens **Politangle School → Teacher resources & pilot**.
2. Teacher previews the available lessons and age bands.
3. Teacher submits work email, school/organisation, country, role and intended age band. No student data is requested.
4. Politangle reviews the request and, when approved, issues a teacher access code.
5. The teacher enters the access code to unlock the operational classroom resources.
6. Students join classroom sessions anonymously with temporary room codes.
7. The teacher sees class-level distributions and aggregate teaching summaries only.

The pilot request itself does not automatically unlock minor-facing classroom operations.
