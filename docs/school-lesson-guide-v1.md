# Politangle School — Lesson Guide v1

Status: **design specification for young-person classroom use.**

This guide defines the first ready-to-run Politangle School lessons. It is designed to support teachers rather than lock them into a script.

## Educational purpose

Politangle School should help young people:

1. distinguish major political families without reducing politics to party stereotypes;
2. understand that political beliefs can overlap and cross traditional left/right boundaries;
3. distinguish political beliefs from political literacy;
4. recognize cross-cutting concepts such as populism, nationalism, authority and pluralism;
5. explain why reasonable people may disagree on political questions;
6. identify the values and trade-offs underneath a political disagreement;
7. understand their own views without being assigned a fixed political identity;
8. discuss differences respectfully using evidence and concepts rather than labels.

The five headline families remain:

- Liberalism
- Conservatism
- Social democracy
- Socialism
- Green politics

Narrower concepts remain contextual or conditional rather than expanding the headline model.

## Audience

Primary product bands are **Junior 12–13** and **Youth 14–18** in secondary education.

Junior uses a separately versioned 16-question candidate bank and guided social-media/political-literacy lessons. Youth uses a plain-language parallel form of all 42 BELIEVE items. The adult form remains unchanged. Neither new form should be described as age-validated until cognitive and empirical testing is complete.

## Teacher use principles

- Ask before explaining when possible.
- Use the class distribution as a discussion starting point.
- Do not praise students for choosing one political pole.
- Do not treat the majority answer as the correct political position.
- For literacy questions, clearly distinguish factual/conceptual correctness from political preference.
- Show competing values where an issue is genuinely contested.
- Invite students to explain reasoning without requiring them to reveal which answer they personally selected.
- Never pressure a student to identify their anonymous response.

## Lesson 1 — Where does our room stand?

**Recommended duration:** 45 minutes

**Purpose:** Introduce anonymous political discussion through selected BELIEVE questions.

### Learning goals

Students should be able to:
- recognize that a class can contain several political viewpoints at once;
- identify the values underneath at least three political disagreements;
- distinguish an issue position from a political identity label.

### Suggested timeline

**0–5 min — Join**
- Teacher opens anonymous classroom.
- Students scan QR / enter code.
- Teacher explains: "I will see how the room answers, never who gave an answer."

**5–8 min — Ground rule**
- Explain that BELIEVE questions have no correct political answer.
- Explain that distributions are for discussion, not ranking.

**8–30 min — Live questions**
Use 6–8 selected questions across different constructs, for example:
- ownership;
- redistribution;
- social change;
- personal autonomy;
- authority/order;
- pluralism;
- ecology;
- national membership or sovereignty.

For each:
1. launch question;
2. watch aggregate response graph;
3. reveal to room when ready;
4. ask one neutral discussion question.

### Neutral prompts

- "What value might lead someone toward the left side of this question?"
- "What value might lead someone toward the right side?"
- "Could someone support both of these goals in different situations?"
- "What additional fact would you want before deciding?"

**30–40 min — Compare two divided questions**
Teacher opens the two most divided distributions.

Ask:
- "Why might this issue split the room more than the others?"
- "Are students disagreeing about goals, or about how to reach the same goal?"

**40–45 min — Reflection**
Students privately answer:
- one idea they understood better;
- one question they now think is more complicated than before.

### Teacher summary

Review:
- most divided questions;
- most consensual questions;
- aggregate construct map if enough items were answered;
- discussion opportunities for next lesson.

## Lesson 2 — Political families without stereotypes

**Recommended duration:** 60 minutes

**Purpose:** Teach the five headline families and test conceptual understanding.

### Learning goals

Students should be able to:
- identify the core features of the five broad families;
- distinguish Social democracy from Socialism;
- understand that Conservatism is not identical to laissez-faire economics;
- understand that Liberalism is internally diverse;
- recognize Green politics as more than a single environmental policy preference.

### Suggested timeline

**0–5 min — Join + warm-up poll**
Run one BELIEVE question with a visibly mixed answer distribution.

**5–20 min — Five-family teaching cards**
Teacher uses Politangle cards for:
- Liberalism
- Conservatism
- Social democracy
- Socialism
- Green politics

Each card should contain:
- one-sentence definition;
- 2–3 characteristic ideas;
- one common misconception;
- one "not necessarily" statement.

Example:

**Conservatism**
- values continuity, inherited institutions and gradual change;
- often gives weight to order, tradition and private property;
- does not automatically mean opposition to welfare or public services.

**Social democracy**
- generally retains predominantly private ownership;
- supports strong welfare/public provision and redistribution;
- is not the same as broad social ownership of productive assets.

**20–40 min — CLASSIFY quiz**
Run 6–9 CLASSIFY questions.

Teacher sees live option distributions.
After each question:
- reveal correct answer;
- show explanation;
- use wrong-answer distribution to identify misconceptions.

**40–52 min — BELIEVE connection**
Run 3–4 BELIEVE questions illustrating why one student can match more than one family.

**52–60 min — Exit quiz**
Run 2–3 UNDERSTAND items.

### Teacher summary

Review:
- CLASSIFY accuracy;
- most-confused family pairs;
- questions that need re-teaching;
- optional aggregate-only PDF.

## Lesson 3 — Think, Feel, Act

**Recommended duration:** 60 minutes

**Purpose:** Help young people see that political judgement has cognitive, affective and behavioral-intention dimensions.

### Learning goals

Students should be able to:
- distinguish THINK, FEEL and ACT as Politangle uses them;
- identify tension without calling it hypocrisy;
- explain why feelings and intended actions may differ from abstract beliefs.

### Suggested timeline

**0–8 min — Explain the triangle**
- THINK = what I judge/believe;
- FEEL = my emotional/intuitive reaction;
- ACT = what I say I would choose/do.

Clarify that ACT is not observed real-world behavior.

**8–35 min — Run 3–4 complete triplets**
Select constructs such as:
- authority/order;
- ecology/growth;
- social change;
- redistribution;
- national membership.

For each triplet, the teacher sees the three aggregate distributions.

**35–50 min — Compare room patterns**
Show aggregate THINK / FEEL / ACT differences.

Prompts:
- "Why might a class intellectually support one principle but feel differently when a concrete case appears?"
- "Does a difference between THINK and ACT automatically mean inconsistency?"
- "What situational information could explain the gap?"

**50–60 min — Reflection**
Students write privately:
- one example where a person's principle and practical choice could reasonably differ.

### Teacher summary

Show:
- aggregate T/F/A comparison;
- constructs with highest aggregate tension;
- no individual tension scores.

## Lesson 4 — Quick 26 classroom lab

**Recommended duration:** 60–75 minutes

**Purpose:** Run the complete Politangle Quick question set as a classroom exploration.

### Learning goals

Students should be able to:
- see the class across several political dimensions;
- understand that politics is multidimensional;
- identify at least two areas where the room is internally diverse;
- interpret broad-family compatibility as compatibility rather than identity.

### Suggested timeline

**0–5 min — Join / privacy explanation**

**5–35 min — Quick 26**
Recommended default: student-paced within the room.
Teacher sees aggregate response progress and distributions.

Alternative: teacher-paced in thematic blocks.

**35–50 min — Class Summary**
Teacher opens:
- aggregate 8-axis map where supported;
- top divided issues;
- top consensual issues;
- five-family aggregate compatibility view where methodologically supported.

Do not display a single "class ideology" label.

**50–65 min — Discussion**
Select 3 distributions for discussion.

**65–75 min — Optional literacy check**
Run 3–5 CLASSIFY / UNDERSTAND items.

## Lesson 5 — Full 42 political profile lab

**Recommended duration:** 90 minutes or 2 x 45 minutes

**Purpose:** Explore the full BELIEVE architecture, including THINK / FEEL / ACT.

### Learning goals

Students should be able to:
- understand the multidimensional political map;
- compare class-level THINK / FEEL / ACT patterns;
- distinguish broad ideology-family compatibility from cross-cutting tendencies;
- discuss why political profiles often contain tensions and overlaps.

### Suggested structure

**Session A**
- join + introduction: 5 min
- Quick 26: 30 min
- discussion: 10 min

**Session B**
- remaining 16: 20 min
- THINK / FEEL / ACT summary: 10 min
- class aggregate map: 5 min
- discussion + reflection: 10 min

## Lesson 6 — Democracy, pluralism and populism

**Recommended duration:** 45–60 minutes

**Purpose:** Separate several concepts that are often collapsed together.

### Learning goals

Students should be able to distinguish:
- elections from liberal-democratic checks;
- populism from authoritarianism;
- nationalism from fascism;
- ordinary Conservatism from radical/extreme-right concepts;
- political disagreement from rejection of pluralism.

### Activity mix

- 3–5 CLASSIFY / UNDERSTAND questions;
- selected BELIEVE items on pluralism, authority/order and populism;
- explanation cards;
- aggregate discussion.

### Key teacher warning

Do not infer that a student or class is "far right" from one answer, from ordinary Conservative views, from patriotism, from welfare skepticism or from a single law-and-order preference.

## Lesson-builder mode

Teacher can create a custom lesson by selecting:
- duration target: 20 / 30 / 45 / 60 / 90 minutes;
- school age/level;
- topics;
- activity type;
- number of BELIEVE questions;
- number of literacy quiz questions;
- whether THINK / FEEL / ACT should appear;
- teacher-paced or student-paced.

Politangle then recommends a sequence but teacher can reorder it.

## Classroom summary design

After any lesson, the teacher should receive a polished summary with sections relevant to what was actually run.

Possible cards:

### Participation
- joined;
- responded;
- questions completed;
- average response completion.

### Room distributions
- most divided;
- most consensual;
- selected question charts.

### Political constructs
- aggregate economy;
- ownership;
- social values;
- authority;
- pluralism;
- sovereignty;
- membership;
- ecology.

### Political families
Where enough questions are available:
- Liberalism aggregate compatibility;
- Conservatism aggregate compatibility;
- Social democracy aggregate compatibility;
- Socialism aggregate compatibility;
- Green politics aggregate compatibility;
- show distribution/bands where supported, not only averages.

### Literacy
- CLASSIFY;
- UNDERSTAND;
- most-missed concepts;
- baseline/post change where used.

### THINK / FEEL / ACT
Only when sufficient Full-42 data exists.

### Next lesson
Politangle recommends 1–3 follow-up teaching activities based on aggregate misconceptions or areas of strong class division.

## PDF lesson report

Optional teacher export should be a polished aggregate-only report with:
- lesson goals;
- activities completed;
- participation;
- key charts;
- concept performance;
- anonymous political distributions;
- discussion prompts;
- teacher notes;
- suggested next lesson.

The PDF is for teaching reflection and curriculum documentation, not student assessment.

## Young-person UX requirements

- one task per screen;
- readable on a phone held in one hand;
- progress visible;
- jargon help available without leaving the question;
- avoid dense academic paragraphs during live questioning;
- explanations should usually be short first, with "Learn more" for depth;
- examples should be understandable to a young person without requiring employment, tax, mortgage or professional-life experience;
- no cartoonish infantilization;
- accessibility and keyboard/screen-reader support required before pilot;
- allow teacher to pause a session when discussion becomes valuable.

## Validation boundary

This guide defines the teaching architecture. It does not prove that:
- all current questions are equally understandable to ages 14–18;
- the post-test is psychometrically equivalent to baseline;
- classroom aggregate political measures are appropriate for every jurisdiction;
- current privacy settings are legally sufficient for minors.

Before real classroom use:

**REQUIRES QUALIFIED LEGAL REVIEW** plus cognitive interviews, accessibility testing and non-production classroom QA.
